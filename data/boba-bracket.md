Updated November 23, 2026

Recommended split: four regional brackets of 6 to 9 shops (Downtown & West, Cambie & South Van, East Van & Burnaby, Richmond), so each session's pickups stay within one area and drinks arrive fresh. Four other splits are below if you'd rather group by brand origin, price tier, rating or specialty.

The 28 shops break down as 13 Taiwanese brands, 6 from mainland China, 1 from the UK and 8 local BC brands. Google ratings run from 3.6 to 4.7, and base drink prices from $5.00 to $11.99.

One scheduling flag: Sharetea Kingsway is listed as closed November 16–19, 2026.

New for Bracket #2: every shop that sells each of six shared drinks, from each shop’s current menu, with the exact item to order and four ways to pick 12 (see “Bracket options by shared drink” below).

## Shop profiles

Grouped by brand origin. Each note covers who the brand is, what it's known for, and anything about the chosen branch worth knowing on event day.

### Taiwanese brands (13)

- **101 Boba (Main St):** Uses 50嵐 branding; [50 Lan](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/50_Lan) started in Tainan in 1994 and has 600+ stores in Taiwan. Reviewers praise fresh, chewy pearls. Counter service, open noon to 9 PM.
- **Chatime (Waterfront):** Founded 2005 in Hsinchu; one of the largest Taiwanese chains, with [80+ Canadian stores](https://chatime.ca/careers). This branch is the most-reviewed on the list (1,126). Self-serve kiosks can glitch.
- **CHICHA San Chen (Oakridge):** Founded 1998; known for tea brewed fresh to order by machine and for fresh milk. Reviewers call it smooth and not overly sweet. Opened its first Vancouver store in February 2025.
- **ChunFunHow (W Broadway):** A Taichung chain known for decorated cups and its artisanal tea house setting. Highest-rated shop on the list (4.7). Open until midnight.
- **CoCo (Kingsway):** Founded 1997; one of the world's largest bubble tea brands, with [5,000+ stores](https://sites.google.com/view/taiwanexpo2024ineurope/home/taste-taiwan-delights). The classic creamer-style benchmark.
- **Gong Cha (Hamilton St):** Founded 2006 in Kaohsiung; [2,000+ stores](https://gongchatea.com.au/?p=28) worldwide. This downtown branch rates 4.6, higher than the Granville location (4.0).
- **Happy Lemon (Robson):** Founded 2006 in Shanghai by Taiwan's Yummy-Town Group; credited with inventing salted cheese tea. No delivery from this branch.
- **MACU Tea (Kerrisdale):** Founded 2008 in Kaohsiung; famous for fresh mango drinks. Three other local MACU stores became Alfa Tea in 2026; Kerrisdale is run by a separate franchisee.
- **Presotea (Richmond):** Founded 2006; brews each tea with an espresso-style machine and runs its own tea factory. 400+ stores worldwide. Uses compostable starch straws.
- **Sharetea (Kingsway):** Founded 1992 in Taipei; the oldest brand on the list. Spacious seating. **Listed as closed November 16–19.**
- **Shiny Tea (Aberdeen Square):** Founded 2005 in Taiwan; reviewers note strong Taiwanese teas at low prices. Closes at 7 PM, among the earliest on the list.
- **The Alley (Hornby):** Founded 2013 by a designer; about [450 stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Alley_%28company%29) worldwide. Makes its own cane syrup and pearls. Some drinks have fixed sugar and ice.
- **Xing Fu Tang (Brentwood):** Founded 2018 in Taipei; specializes in stir-fried brown sugar pearls made in front of customers. Reviewers compare it to shops in Taiwan.

### Mainland Chinese brands (6)

- **Ben Gong's Tea (Grimmer St):** Founded around 2014; markets organic, additive-free tea and handmade pearls. Lowest-rated with Boba Run (3.6). Open 11 AM to 11 PM.
- **Chahalo (Richmond):** Founded 2016; 1,000+ stores. Tea-forward, modern tea house design. Lines were long at its BC opening.
- **Hey Tea (Richmond):** Founded 2012 in Jiangmen; credited with inventing cheese tea. About [4,000 stores](https://www.hsgcap.com/companies/heytea/). Most expensive drink on the list ($11.99). Expect long evening waits.
- **SunTea (City Square):** A Chinese brand with 5+ stores across Metro Vancouver. This branch is a counter inside SunGiven grocery; newest, with only 20 reviews.
- **Sweet7 (Robson):** Founded 2006 in China; 1,500+ stores, known for mango drinks. Robson opened in 2024 as its first North American store.
- **TeaByDo (Richmond):** Known as Cha Panda outside English-speaking markets; 9,000+ stores. Opened August 2026; no seating, and no tapioca on the menu.

### UK brand (1)

- **CHAYAN (Richmond):** London-born brand serving Chinese-style tea with fresh milk, known for its black tea frappe. Confirmed it can add pearls. Busiest on weekend afternoons.

### Local BC brands (8)

- **Alfa Tea Concept (Burnaby):** Opened in 2026 in a former MACU store, with a near-identical menu. Reviewers warn of long weekend waits.
- **Boba Run (Oakridge):** Vancouver's first Korean-inspired bubble tea shop, with two locations. Uses fresh milk; boba and dairy alternatives cost extra.
- **Bubble World (Kingsway):** Long-running local chain that also serves Taiwanese food. Cheapest drink on the list ($5.00). Open past midnight.
- **Choo Tea (Crystal Mall):** Small local chain that started at Aberdeen Square in 2019. Known for taro and purple yam drinks.
- **Money Tea (Crystal Mall):** Independent shop founded in Richmond and known for its lucky-cat decor. Closes at 8 PM (7 PM Wednesdays).
- **Paragon Tea Room (Cambie):** Founded 2019; a matcha specialist using teaspresso machines and oat milk by default. Closes at 7 PM (6 PM Sundays).
- **Pearl Fever (Coquitlam):** Founded 2002 in Coquitlam; 7 locations across the Lower Mainland. Farthest pickup on the list.
- **The Bubble Tea Shop (Olympic Village):** Local shop known for egg waffles and duo cups. Closes at 8 PM most days.

## Recommendation

Go with Option 1 (by area): four regional sessions, then a Grand Final of the four regional champions. Since drinks are bought once, short pickup routes matter more than anything else for a fair taste test.

| Session | Shops | Format | Drinks (3 per shop) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Downtown & West | 7 | 1 group of 4 + 1 group of 3, winners meet in a final | 21 |
| Cambie & South Van | 6 | 2 groups of 3, winners meet in a final | 18 |
| East Van, Burnaby & Coquitlam | 9 | 3 groups of 3, the 3 winners ranked head to head | 27 |
| Richmond | 6 | 2 groups of 3, winners meet in a final | 18 |

That's 96 drinks across five sessions. Hold the East Van session after November 19 so Sharetea is open.

If you'd rather run fewer, bigger sessions with more drivers, Option 2 (origin and era) is the best alternative: three brackets of 8 to 11, each with a clear storyline.

### Four ways to pick 12

- **Most new:** every shop nobody has tasted yet, highest rated first. If that’s fewer than 12, Bracket #1’s finalists fill in (champion first), then other Bracket #1 shops by rating.
- **Rematch:** every Bracket #1 finalist that sells the drink (ChunFunHow, Shiny Tea, 101 Boba, Bubble World), then new shops by rating.
- **Fewest areas:** whole pickup areas, biggest first, until there are 12, then the lowest-rated shops are dropped (never emptying an area). Fewer areas means fewer drivers and fresher drinks, the lesson from Bracket #1’s late deliveries.
- **Top rated:** the 12 highest Google ratings, ties broken by review count.

In each table, ✓ marks the shops a method picks.

## Methodology

The list and categories came from a seven-step process run between October 20 and November 15, 2026; step 8 was added on November 23 for the Bracket #2 options. Anyone repeating these steps with the same rules should land on a similar list; the judgment calls are flagged so they can be challenged.

### Steps

1. **Initial discovery.** Searched Google Places for "bubble tea" in six areas: Downtown, Kitsilano, Cambie/Oakridge, Kingsway/Main, Richmond and Metrotown. Each search returns about 10 places, which gave 45 shops.
2. **Keep one branch per brand.** Brands with several branches were cut to one, initially the highest-rated. Three were later swapped for practical reasons: SunTea to City Square (closer), Boba Run to Oakridge (its menu could be verified), and Pearl Fever to Coquitlam (the UBC branch is closed weekends).
3. **Exclude shops that can't make a comparable drink.** Removed shops whose core product isn't a dairy milk tea with tapioca: NUTTEA (nut milk), CocoBay (coconut base), YOTEA (fruit tea), Dollar Boba (self-serve) and Real Fruit (fruit-focused). The organizer removed UG Tea (floral oolong) and tabled Milksha (possible rebrand to Mika), Hanlin Tea (no milk tea on its menu) and Molly Tea (white boba only).
4. **Sweep for missed chains.** Ran a second round of Places searches for about 30 brand names and 10 more neighbourhoods (Chinatown, Commercial Drive, Kerrisdale, UBC, Robson, Aberdeen, Brentwood, Lougheed, Steveston, Joyce), then checked 2025–2026 local food news for openings and closures. A brand counted as a chain with 2+ Metro Vancouver locations, or as a global brand with one. Closed brands (Tiger Sugar, Machi Machi, Yi Fang, Wushiland, Comebuy) were dropped.
5. **Pick and verify the drink.** The rule: each shop's plain black-tea milk tea with tapioca pearls, ordered large, 50% sugar, less ice. If pearls aren't included, add them. If there's no plain black milk tea, take the closest option in this order: black tea base, then oolong, then anything else, and flag it. Each drink was confirmed on that branch's Google Maps menu (signed in, so the full menu shows), on the brand's own site (CoCo, Sharetea, The Alley), or by the organizer calling the shop (CHAYAN, Chahalo, Ben Gong's, Molly Tea).
6. **Profile each shop.** Ratings, review counts, addresses and hours came from Google Places on November 15. Brand origin, founding year and store count came from brand sites, Wikipedia and local food press (Daily Hive, Noms Magazine, Richmond News, Foodgressing). Where sources disagreed, the most-cited figure was used. "Known for" came from menu highlights, review summaries and press coverage.
7. **Build the categories.** The constraints: no bracket over 12, and every shop in exactly one bracket. Only attributes available for all 28 shops were used: area, origin and era, price, rating, and specialty. Cut points were set to give balanced sizes, then adjusted by hand where needed.
8. **Match drinks across menus (November 23, 2026).** Read every shop's menu from its Google Maps listing. Where the listing's menu was thin (Boba Run, Pearl Fever, TeaByDo, The Bubble Tea Shop), used the most recent menu photo posted to the listing. Grouped drinks with the same or nearly the same name and counted the shops for each. For each drink at least 12 shops share, listed every shop and applied four selection rules (most new, rematch, fewest areas, top rated) to pick 12. Every item in the tables was checked against the menu it came from.

### Decision rules behind each option

| Option | Rule | Cut points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. By area | Group by city or neighbourhood cluster of the chosen branch | Downtown & West, Cambie & South Van, East Van/Burnaby/Coquitlam, Richmond; 101 Boba moved to Cambie & South Van to balance sizes |

| 5. By specialty | The shop's signature product | Classic & boba: signature is a milk tea or its pearls. Tea-forward: a tea variety or brewing method. Fruit & foam: fruit or cheese/milk foam. Creative: a novelty format. Tea-forward and Creative share one bracket to keep sizes balanced |

Option 1 is recommended because drinks are bought once and pearls harden within hours, so short pickup routes matter most for a fair test.

### Judgment calls open to challenge

- **What counts as a chain.** Counting global brands with one local shop (Happy Lemon, TeaByDo, Chahalo) is a choice; a strict local-only rule would drop them.
- **Which branch.** Highest-rated was the default, but ratings differ between branches of the same brand (Gong Cha downtown 4.6 vs Granville 4.0), so branch choice can shift results.
- **Closest-drink substitutions.** Xing Fu Tang (brown sugar), Boba Run (Hong Kong style), Sweet7 and Paragon (oolong), Chahalo (dark tea) and TeaByDo (coconut jelly) aren't true like-for-like drinks. TeaByDo's menu board (photo from September 2026) now lists a handmade brown sugar pearl milk tea and a brown sugar pearl topping, both marked sold out that day, so pearls may be available after all.
- **Drink matching (Bracket #2 options).** Drinks were matched by name, not recipe: “Green Milk Tea” counts as jasmine green milk tea, and lattes, fresh-milk teas and oat-milk versions count as milk teas. Each table names the shop's own item so the differences are visible.
- **Founding years.** Sources conflict for The Alley (2013 vs 2015) and Happy Lemon (Taipei vs Shanghai). ChunFunHow's founding year wasn't found.
- **Specialty tags.** These are judgment calls from reviews and menus. Gong Cha, for example, could fit Classic & boba as easily as Fruit & foam.
- **Coverage.** Places searches cap at about 10 results each, so a small chain in an unsearched neighbourhood could still be missing.

### Data limits

- Ratings and review counts change daily. SunTea City Square's 4.5 rests on 20 reviews.
- Menu prices on Google can lag in-store prices. Prices for Chahalo and Pearl Fever weren't captured.
- Some brand facts come from search-result excerpts rather than full pages; the Sources list links each one.
- The drink-matched options use menus read November 23, 2026. Seasonal items come and go: MACU Tea's menu had no mango drinks that day, and Chatime's and Presotea's included limited-time drinks.
